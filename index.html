My deepest boundary has always been loyalty. Not perfection. Not constant attention. Just honesty, reassurance, and emotional consistency.
Some past connections treated commitment like a temporary feeling, always looking over the fence for “something better” or “more compatible.”
Those experiences changed something in me. They created pain on both sides and slowly taught my heart to protect itself before it gets too comfortable.
That is why I take trust seriously now.
Not because I want control, but because I understand how damaging emotional uncertainty can become when feelings are genuine.
I also recognize something about myself with more honesty these days: when I care about someone, I attach deeply.
Being far from familiarity and trying to build a life in a place that still feels unfamiliar has made emotional connection feel heavier for me than it may for others.
In my culture, effort matters a lot. When someone truly likes a person, they pursue with intention, consistency, and visible interest until the other person feels seen and valued. Small efforts, remembering details, checking in, and showing up consistently soften my heart more than grand words ever could.
During the trust-building stage, I tend to invest emotionally with sincerity. So when a connection disappears after the infatuation fades, it leaves behind a quiet kind of exhaustion that is difficult to explain.
If my fears, caution, or emotional intensity ever felt overwhelming, I can understand that perspective, and I am sorry for the weight it may have carried.
Still, I am learning that love should not feel like survival mode. The right connection will not make loyalty feel old-fashioned or emotional safety feel like “too much.” 🌙
